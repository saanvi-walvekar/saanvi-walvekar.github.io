There are two common designs.

**Ionization detectors** hold a speck of americium-241 between two metal plates. It gives off alpha particles that knock electrons off air molecules, so the air between the plates carries a small, steady current. Smoke particles drifting in stick to those charged molecules, the current drops, and the alarm goes off.

**Photoelectric detectors** shine a light across a dark chamber, with a sensor sitting off to one side where the beam cannot reach it. Smoke scatters the light, some of it lands on the sensor, and that triggers the alarm.

Ionization alarms tend to react faster to fast, flaming fires. Photoelectric alarms tend to react faster to slow, smouldering ones. That is why many homes use both.
